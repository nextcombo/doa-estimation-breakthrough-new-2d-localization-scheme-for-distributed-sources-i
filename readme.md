Abstract: This paper proposes an ESPRIT-based method for signal parameter estimation, which is used for two-dimensional (2-D) positioning of non-coherent distributed (ID) sources in large-scale multi-input multi-output (MIMO) systems. The traditional ESPRIT method is only suitable for one-dimensional positioning of non-coherent distributed sources, while this method achieves the estimation of nominal azimuth angle, elevation angle arrival direction, and angle spread by constructing a signal subspace. This estimator has a closed-form expression without searching over the entire feasible domain, thus significantly reducing computational complexity compared to traditional two-dimensional estimation methods. Analysis indicates that the estimation performance of this method will improve when using large-scale MIMO systems. This paper also derives the approximate Cramér-Rao bound (CRB) for this estimator for two-dimensional positioning. Numerical results show that although the performance of this estimation method is comparable to that of traditional two-dimensional estimators, it significantly reduces computational complexity.
In the past two decades, Multi-Input Multi-Output (MIMO) technology has been a breakthrough in the wireless communication field, offering more degrees of freedom and significantly enhancing system data rates and link reliability. In recent years, Massive MIMO (also known as Massive Antenna MIMO) has attracted increasing attention due to its unprecedented potential for high spectral efficiency. In massive MIMO systems, base stations are equipped with hundreds of antennas, serving dozens of user terminals simultaneously. When the angle spread is not wide enough, system performance will significantly degrade, necessitating beamforming methods to achieve directional antenna gains. Additionally, due to array aperture limitations, the antenna arrays in massive MIMO systems must adopt multidimensional structures, and beamforming must operate in the two-dimensional space corresponding to azimuth and elevation angles. The system performance based on beamforming is closely related to the accuracy of angle parameter estimation, and estimation errors can lead to a significant reduction in output signal-to-noise ratio. Moreover, as the number of antennas increases, the impact of estimation errors becomes more pronounced. Therefore, this paper focuses on the problem of two-dimensional positioning of distributed sources in massive MIMO systems, requiring simultaneous estimation of azimuth and elevation angle-related parameters.
Source positioning (i.e., direction of arrival estimation) has been a research hotspot in the field of signal processing for decades. The point source model is suitable for line-of-sight transmission scenarios, while the distributed source model corresponds to multipath transmission scenarios, which better align with the actual conditions of cellular wireless systems. Distributed sources are divided into coherent distributed (CD) and non-coherent distributed (ID) sources, respectively applicable to slow time-varying channels and fast time-varying channels. In cellular mobile communication systems, fast time-varying channels can better reflect the actual environment, but the research on the localization of non-coherent distributed sources is still insufficient. Traditional parameterized methods are mostly suitable for one-dimensional localization of non-coherent distributed sources, but when extended to two-dimensional scenarios, they face high computational complexity and the need for exhaustive search issues. The subspace-based ESPRIT method, although outstanding in one-dimensional localization, has limitations such as coupling angle parameters and the need for searching in two-dimensional scenarios. Therefore, this paper proposes a two-dimensional localization ESPRIT method suitable for large-scale MIMO systems, addressing the shortcomings of existing methods.
Method Introduction: The system model considers the use of an uniform rectangular array (URA) with a number of antennas, and the number of antennas in both the x-direction and y-direction. All user terminals' transmission signals are within the same frequency band, and under scattering conditions, the received signal by the antenna array is composed of multipath signals from each user terminal and additive noise. The azimuth and elevation angles of arrival for each user terminal can be represented as the sum of nominal values and zero-mean random angular deviations, with the standard deviation of the angular deviations being the angular spread. The positioning task estimates the nominal two-dimensional arrival directions and angular spreads for each user terminal through receiving signal snapshots.
Location method based on ESPRIT 1
Signal Subspace Construction: Approximate the received signal as a product of the array response matrix and the signal vector, by expanding the first-order Taylor series near the nominal arrival direction. Through eigenvalue decomposition of the covariance matrix of the received signal, separate the signal subspace from the noise subspace, with the signal subspace approximately coinciding with the array response matrix.
2  
Subarray partitioning and linear relationship construction: Divide a uniform rectangular array into three subarrays, with subarrays 2 and 3 being displaced by distances d along the x and y directions respectively relative to subarray 1. Derive the array response matrices for each subarray, revealing that they exhibit a linear relationship, which is related to the two-dimensional nominal arrival direction.
3  
Estimator design: Based on the Total Least Square (TLS) criterion, the transformation matrix is estimated using the selected signal subspace of the subarray. By performing eigenvalue decomposition on the transformation matrix and combining it with an eigenvalue matching algorithm, we obtain estimates of the nominal arrival directions in two dimensions. Based on the estimated results of the array response matrix, the angular spread is estimated by matching the covariance matrix.

The method does not restrict the spacing between adjacent antennas, enabling decoupling of two-dimensional angle parameters and demonstrating feasibility for two-dimensional positioning.
